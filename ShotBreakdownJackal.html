01

The decision

“I had 400mm mounted. I used roughly 200mm instead.”

Not because I lacked reach. Because reach wasn’t what the photograph needed. At 400mm this jackal is a clean, tight portrait, nothing wrong with that. Pulled back, the place becomes part of the subject.

02

Composition

THE FOREST BUILT THE FRAME

The arching trees become a frame the forest built on its own. The jackal is small enough that the habitat does half the storytelling.

03

Same morning, same call

EGRET · PULLED BACK

The egret stayed roughly the same size whether I used 400mm or pulled back. What changed was everything around it: the dark, textured forest that gives a white bird somewhere to exist.

04

Before you zoom to max

CostIf full reach crops out the habitat, the light or the context, that’s a cost, not just a benefit.
SizeA subject doesn’t need to be large to be the focal point. Contrast, light and position can do it.
RangeTreat your zoom range as a range, not a target.
GearA 100-400 on a crop body is real reach. Most limits people blame on gear are decisions not yet made.
