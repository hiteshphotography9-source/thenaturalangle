## 1. Why Are There No Tigers in Sri Lanka? (80 s) | READY
**File:** video/NoTigersSriLanka_V2_HQ_90MB.mp4
**Title:** Why Are There No Tigers in Sri Lanka? The Answer Isn't Distance
**Description:**
India has tigers. Sri Lanka is under 50 km away and has none. This Short follows the evidence: a shallow strait, a sea that fell about 120 m in the last Ice Age, a land bridge, and two tentatively identified fossils.

Fact vs hypothesis: no wild tigers live in Sri Lanka today (fact). A toe bone and a tooth were tentatively identified as tiger in 2005 (hypothesis, still debated). Why tigers did not persist on the island is not proven; the leopard became the top predator.

One more fact: Sri Lanka also had a lion subspecies that vanished before about 37,000 years ago.

Sources: Manamendra-Arachchi et al. (2005), Raffles Bulletin of Zoology Suppl. 12; Ceylon Journal of Science (Sri Lankan mammals); Adam's Bridge / Palk Strait; Miththapala et al. (1996); IUCN Red List (tiger).

## 2. Tiger Island: Tigers Can Swim. So Why Aren't They on Every Island? (100.7 s, with VO) | READY
**File:** video/tiger_island/TigerIsland_Final_HQ.mp4
**Title options:** 1. Tigers Can Swim. So Why Aren't They on Every Island? (recommended) 2. Can a Tiger Swim Across the Sea? The Answer Surprised Me 3. Why Tigers Never Reached Lombok (Just 20 km From Bali)
**Thumbnail:** Tiger_Island_cover.jpg (portrait)
**Description:**
Tigers are strong swimmers. Sundarbans tigers cross several water channels every day, and Malayan tigers once swam the Johor Strait into Singapore. So why aren't there tigers on every island?

Because swimming moves one tiger. A population needs a male and a female, a big territory (a tigress uses roughly 16 to 180 sq km in Indian studies), enough prey, and in the long run hundreds of tigers.

That is why tigers walked into Bali over Ice Age land, but never crossed the deep Lombok Strait, only about 20 to 35 km wide. Tiger bones have been found on Borneo and Palawan, and tentatively in Sri Lanka, but none of these islands has wild tigers today. Singapore's tigers arrived in the 1800s and the last one was shot in 1930.

Watch the Sri Lanka Short next: why there are no tigers in Sri Lanka.

## 4. Dhole: The Predator People Forget (84 s, with VO) | READY (add binocular link and photographer name)
**File:** video/dhole/reel/Dhole_Reel_Final_HQ.mp4 | **Thumbnail:** Dhole_Reel_cover_portrait.jpg
**Title options**
1. India's Forgotten Predator Is Showing Up Again (recommended)
2. Dhole: The Wild Dog That Chases Tigers Off Their Kill
3. Gone for 30 Years. Then a Camera Trap Caught This.

**Thumbnail (portrait):** Dhole_Reel_cover_portrait.jpg

**Description**
Tiger and leopard get the attention. The dhole, India's wild dog, is the predator people forget. It weighs 10 to 20 kg, hunts in packs of 5 to 12 (sometimes 30+), whistles to talk in thick forest, and brings down prey many times its size. Packs have been recorded chasing tigers off their kills (rare, documented by researchers).

It has vanished from over 75% of its historic range, was hunted as "vermin" in India until the Wildlife Protection Act of 1972, and only about 949 to 2,215 breeding adults are estimated worldwide (2015 estimate).

Now new records are coming in: Assam's Kaziranga-Karbi Anglong landscape (camera trap on 31 Oct 2022, about 30 years after dholes were thought gone there; WII study published June 2025), Manipur's first ever camera-trap record (Shirui, July 2026), and Ratapani Tiger Reserve's first record (Jan 2026, as reported). A comeback? Not yet. Every new record is a reason to protect that forest.

**File:** video/pilibhit_short/
**Title:** How Did This Forest Double Its Tigers in 4 Years? | Pilibhit
**Description:**
Pilibhit Tiger Reserve went from about 25 tigers in 2014 to 65 in 2018 and won the first TX2 award in 2020. What actually happened?
Documented: reserve notified June 2014 (about 730 sq km); NTCA credits patrol technology, habitat and grassland work and action against poachers; the 2018 figure is 57 resident plus 8 transit tigers; tigers move across the India-Nepal border.
Uncertain: how much of the rise is breeding vs movement, and the 2010 and 2013 counts.
The catch: the reserve is narrow and bordered by sugarcane; WTI recorded 26 big-cat conflict cases (18 tigers) around Pilibhit in 2024.
