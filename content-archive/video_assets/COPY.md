## 1. Why are there no tigers in Sri Lanka? (80 s)
**Instagram**
India has wild tigers. Sri Lanka, under 50 km away, has none. Why?

It isn't distance. In the last Ice Age the sea was about 120 m lower, and India and Sri Lanka were joined by land for more than half of the last 500,000 years. A toe bone (about 16,500 years old) and a tooth found in Sri Lanka were tentatively identified as tiger in 2005, but that ID is still debated.

Best-supported explanation: the sea didn't keep tigers out. The island may not have kept them in.

What do you think happened to Sri Lanka's tigers? Save this for your next wildlife debate.

**YouTube description**
India has tigers. Sri Lanka is under 50 km away and has none. This Short follows the evidence: a shallow strait, a sea that fell about 120 m in the last Ice Age, a land bridge, and two tentatively identified fossils.

Fact vs hypothesis: no wild tigers live in Sri Lanka today (fact). A toe bone and a tooth were tentatively identified as tiger in 2005 (hypothesis, still debated). Why tigers did not persist on the island is not proven; the leopard became the top predator.

One more fact I couldn't fit in: Sri Lanka also had a lion subspecies that vanished before about 37,000 years ago.

## 3. Pilibhit: how a forest doubled its tigers (71 s)
**Instagram**
25 tigers in 2014. 65 in 2018. How does a forest do that in four years?

Pilibhit lost tigers first: reports put the count at 40+ in 2010 and around 23 by 2013, with questions about poaching. After it became a tiger reserve in June 2014, NTCA credits patrol technology (M-STrIPES), habitat and grassland work, and strict action against poachers.

But the 65 includes 8 transit tigers, and tigers move across the Nepal border, so how much came from breeding and how much from movement can't be said exactly.

It won the first TX2 award in 2020. Now the test is the edge: a narrow forest beside sugarcane, where WTI recorded 26 big-cat conflict cases around Pilibhit in 2024.

**YouTube title:** How Did This Forest Double Its Tigers in 4 Years? | Pilibhit

**YouTube description**
Pilibhit Tiger Reserve went from about 25 tigers in 2014 to 65 in 2018 and won the first TX2 award in 2020. What actually happened?

What is documented: the reserve was notified in June 2014 (about 730 sq km); NTCA credits patrol technology, habitat and grassland improvement and action against poachers; the 2018 figure is 57 resident plus 8 transit tigers; tigers move across the India-Nepal border.
What is uncertain: how much of the rise is breeding and how much is movement, and the 2010 and 2013 counts (sources differ).
The catch: the reserve is narrow and bordered by sugarcane; WTI recorded 26 big-cat conflict cases (18 tigers) in the Pilibhit Social Forestry Division in 2024.
