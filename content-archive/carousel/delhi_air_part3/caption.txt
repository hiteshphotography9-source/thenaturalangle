That is not fog. I thought it was morning mist at Surajpur wetland. It was smog. This is Delhi smog season, from my own vlog.

What it did to my morning:
- The horizon was gone. Water, palms and sky turned into one flat grey.
- My lens kept hunting for focus through the haze.
- Even when a bird was sharp, the sky behind it was a white wall. No colour, no mood.
- The haze thinned very slowly. By 10 am the light was already harsh.
- I saw few raptors and few ducks. In my view the smog is part of why. That is my view from the field, not a study.
- I went home early, hoping to come back when it eases.

SAVE THIS for smog days (slide 8): 1) Manual focus on a hard edge. 2) Add +1 EV, haze fools the meter. 3) Get closer, less air in the frame. 4) Use the haze for sun orbs and silhouettes.

Numbers from Part 1 (slide 5): Najafgarh Jheel 27,673 (2021) to 10,592 (2022). Sultanpur 9,026 (2023) to 2,686 (2024). Different wetlands from Surajpur, counts vary by year and method, and Haryana's forest department says Sultanpur totals have risen. Not proof that smog caused it.

But the birds are still there. A pair of spotted owlets that same day, clear eyes in clear light. This is what we stand to lose.
