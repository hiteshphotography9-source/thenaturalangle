Smog is not only in our lungs. Delhi's vets say animals are breathing it too. Here is what we know, and what we don't.

What vets are seeing:
- One Delhi clinic handled about 150 cases of breathing, eye and stomach trouble in pets and birds since November (Dec 2025). That is a clinic count, not a census.
- A Delhi vet says respiratory distress is the most common problem he sees in smog season.
- Dogs, cats and rabbits breathe closer to the ground, where PM2.5 is higher.

What studies say: it is small and mixed. A 2018 Taiwan study (230 dogs, 118 cats, indoor air) found PM2.5 above 35 was linked to about 4x higher odds of respiratory disease in cats, but not in dogs.

What nobody has measured: Delhi's wild animals. Asola Bhatti has leopards (8 in a 2022 survey). We found no study of what smog does to them. Researchers call the impact on wild animals "complex and under-studied", and warn of slower damage: fewer successful breedings, changes in genetic health, disrupted behaviour. None of it shows up right away.

If you have a pet, vets advise: limit time outdoors, keep fresh water ready, and see a vet quickly for breathing trouble, eye irritation or odd behaviour. Save this for the next smog week.
