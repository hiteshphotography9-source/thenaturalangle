27,673 birds counted at Najafgarh Jheel in 2021. 10,592 in 2022.
Sultanpur: 9,026 in 2023, 2,686 in 2024.

Delhi's AQI hit 367 this week. Every winter, thousands of migratory birds settle in these wetlands, and they breathe the same air we do.

What the evidence says:
- Fine particles can inflame bird lungs (research review, 2025).
- One east Delhi clinic treated about 25 birds with irritated eyes in a few weeks. That is not a census.
- Delhi's 22 km of the Yamuna is the most polluted of India's 311 river stretches (CEEW).

What it does not say: that air alone caused the fall. Dry wetlands, dirty water, dust and building, and warmer winters matter too, and experts still debate the share. Counts also change with year, rain and method. Haryana's forest department says Sultanpur's totals have risen.
