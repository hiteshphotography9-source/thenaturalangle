# Machli carousel: caption, alt text, notes

## Instagram caption
She lost her fangs to a crocodile.
She still ruled the lakes.

This is Machli, T-16, the tigress of Ranthambore. Born around 1996 or 97. Named "fish" for a mark on her face. She killed a 14-foot crocodile and lost her canines doing it. She raised cubs, welcomed the world to the water's edge, and on 18 August 2016 she stopped eating and walked away from her territory.

They cremated her in a public ceremony. The lakes are still there. So are her descendants.

Which tiger's story should I tell next? Tell me below.
Save this before your next Ranthambore safari.

Photos are mine, from Ranthambore. They are not Machli. Facts are as reported by the sources on the last slide. Where sources differ, I say so.

## Why this structure
Audience problem: people scroll past "famous tiger" posts because they are facts without a story.
Core belief: the best wildlife stories are real, and a real story needs honest sourcing.
Potential: discovery (a story people send to others) and authority (labelled, sourced).
Reel or YouTube: yes. Slides 1 to 9 map straight to a 45 s Reel. A 6 to 8 min YouTube documentary with real Machli photos and the Ranthambore map is a strong long video.

## Fact check (everything is "reported", none of it comes from me)
| Line | Status |
|---|---|
| "Machli" means fish, from a fish-shaped mark on her face | Widely reported. |
| Born around 1996 or 97 | Sources differ (May 1996, spring 1997, or no record). Shown as "around 1996 or 97". |
| 14-foot crocodile, she killed it, near Raj Bagh, around 2003 | Reported by Scroll and tourism sources. The 2003 date is one tourism source. Shown as "around 2003". |
| She lost her canines in that fight | One source says canines. Others say she lost teeth in later years. Shown as "cost her her canine teeth". Check. |
| 11 cubs in five litters, 1999 to 2006 | Reported by several sources, some say fewer litters. Marked "reported; sources differ". |
| Most photographed tigress in the world | Common label, not an official record. Used as the headline claim. Soften if you prefer ("often called the most photographed"). |
| TOFT Lifetime Achievement Award and a postal stamp | Several sources for both. Stamp date conflicts (2013 or later). Marked "reported". |
| Daughters Sundari and Krishna | Reported; Sundari has her own Wikipedia page. |
| "More than half of Ranthambore's tigers descend from her" | One source, an estimate. Shown as "it is said". |
| 18 August 2016, age 19 (some say 20) | NBC and the park say 19; others say 20. |
| Stopped eating, left her territory toward farmland, food left | Deccan Herald and NBC reports. Shown as reported. |
| Cremated in a public ceremony with Hindu rites | Wikipedia. |
