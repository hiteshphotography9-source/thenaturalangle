# Panna story Short: research + VO (draft 1)

## Verified facts (source, confidence)
- Panna NP 1981, tiger reserve 1994 (Wikipedia, reserve site). Area: core 576.13 + buffer ~1,022 = ~1,598 sq km (MP forest portal). Medium: other pages give other splits.
- Feb/Jun 2009: reserve declared with no tigers; 40+ tigers about 6 years earlier (Tiger Safari/ press note). High on "2009 zero", medium on "40+".
- Mar 2009: T1 (Bandhavgarh) + T2 (Kanha) tigresses brought in. Nov 2009: male T3 (Pench) walked out, tracked and brought back, re-released 26 Dec 2009 (Panna press note). High.
- T1: 4 cubs Apr 2010 (2 survived). T2: 4 cubs late 2010 (all survived). High (reserve note).
- Wiley 2024 study: 18 females, 120 cubs, 45 litters since 2009; 59 tigers in 2021 (ResearchGate abstract). High.
- Reserve yearly review 2021: 35+ tigers dispersed outside PTR over time. High.
- P213-22 walked 99 km to unprotected land and raised cubs (Mongabay 2021). High.
- Ken-Betwa: Daudhan dam 77 m high; PM launched Dec 2024; est. 57.21 sq km of reserve submerged, 10.07% of critical tiger habitat (Current Science 2021 study). Other estimates 41 to 100 sq km. LABEL AS ESTIMATE ON SCREEN.
- NOT USED: "officials faked pugmarks" (blog allegation only). "T3 walked 450 km" (single source).

## VO English (~80 s)
2009. Panna told India it had no tigers left.
Six years earlier, this reserve held over forty.
Poaching and neglect emptied it.
Then came a risky idea. March 2009: one tigress from Bandhavgarh, one from Kanha.
Then a male from Pench. He walked out of the reserve, heading home. Forest teams tracked him for weeks and brought him back.
It worked. Next April, the first cubs. Four from T1, two survived. Four from T2, all four survived.
By 2021, 59 tigers. One hundred and twenty cubs from just 18 females.
And now the problem nobody planned for. Over thirty-five tigers have already walked out of the reserve. One young female covered 99 kilometres to find land of her own.
Panna is full.
And a new dam is coming. The Ken-Betwa link will bring water to Bundelkhand. One study says it could drown about 57 square kilometres of this reserve.
From zero to full in fifteen years. So the question is no longer can tigers come back. It is where they will live.
Water or forest? Where do you stand?

Phir Pench se ek male. Wo reserve se nikal pada, apne ghar ki taraf. Forest team ne hafton tak peecha kiya aur wapas laayi.
Kaam kar gaya. Agle saal April mein pehle cubs. T1 ke chaar, do bache. T2 ke chaar, chaaron bache.

Ab ek aisi problem jiski kisi ne planning nahi ki thi. Paintis se zyada tiger reserve se bahar nikal chuke hain. Ek jawaan tigress ne apni jagah dhoondhne ke liye 99 kilometre tay kiye.
Panna bhar gaya hai.
Aur ab ek dam aa raha hai. Ken-Betwa link Bundelkhand ko paani dega. Ek study kehti hai ye reserve ka lagbhag 57 square kilometre doob sakta hai.
Zero se full, pandrah saal mein. Sawaal ab ye nahi ki tiger wapas aa sakte hain ya nahi. Sawaal ye hai ki wo rahenge kahan.
Paani ya jungle? Aap kahan khade hain?
