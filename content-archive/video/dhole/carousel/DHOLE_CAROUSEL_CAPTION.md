In parts of India, the dhole was written off for decades.

Then on one October night in 2022, a camera trap in Assam's Kaziranga-Karbi Anglong landscape caught a lone dhole. Six photos. The first proof there in about 30 years.

In July 2026, Manipur got its first ever camera-trap record of a dhole, in the Shirui hills. The camera spot was picked by a former hunter who now protects these forests. Madhya Pradesh's Ratapani Tiger Reserve logged its first record in January 2026.

Good news. Not a recovery yet. Dholes are gone from over 75% of their historic range, and only about 949 to 2,215 breeding adults are left worldwide (2015 estimate). Every new record is a reason to protect that forest.

Save this for your next safari. Dholes vanish fast, so keep your binoculars ready.
